# 01 — Research methodology and epistemology
Status: proposed.

## Separate warrants
A source edition is an artifact; a linguistic analysis is a scholarly claim about it; an interpretation is a semantic hypothesis; a logical formalization is a representation of that hypothesis; a proof is a checked derivation under assumptions. Never promote one warrant to another by naming it Quranic.

Maintain a dependency graph with source spans, reading, analysis IDs, interpretation IDs, contextual commitments, and formal rule IDs. A proof whose dependencies change is stale until rechecked.

## Evidence classes
Q: Quran text and explicitly cited cross-verse evidence. L: linguistic evidence (grammar, lexicon, usage). E: external interpretive evidence (tafsir, historical reports, hadith, theological schools). F: formal definitions/rules. H: hadith under evaluation. An interpretation may need L and F; these are not revelation. External evidence is recorded and profiled, not silently admitted into a Quran-only comparison.

## Profiles
- Q-text: only textual facts, no claim that text alone uniquely determines semantics.
- Q-linguistic: Quran spans plus declared language and logic assumptions; excludes hadith/tafsir-derived substantive premises unless independently warranted and recorded.
- Contextual: allows named external sources, with separate outputs.
- Comparative: evaluates a registered set of independently reviewed profiles.

These profiles are proposed methodological choices; their labels do not certify theological authority. Prevent circular reasoning by recording both direct and indirect dependence on the hadith under examination and related reports; a source firewall flags shared dependencies. An independent reviewer evaluates whether evidence is genuinely independent.

## Workflow
Identify verse/speech act and speaker → analyze grammar and lexical senses → locate related passages and context → propose alternatives and explicit scope → formalize → check interpretation fidelity → check logic → review and publish with caveats. Preserve rejected and disputed interpretations with reasons and revision history. Never force consensus by majority vote alone.

## Interpretive risks
Distinguish quoted speech from authorial assertions (e.g. a speaker's claim inside a narrative); commands from descriptions; historical addressees from universals; absolute from qualified language; exceptions, negation scope, time, metaphor, and rhetorical questions. Do not assume chronology or abrogation; record competing proposals with evidence. Do not conflate two words based on shared root.

## Review protocol
Independent Arabic-language and Islamic-studies reviews plus formal-methods review. Reviewer agreement is not proof of correct exegesis. Log conflicts, alternative parses, terminology choices, and scope. Use external scholars and document relevant methodological diversity. AI outputs remain draft annotations until checked against source evidence.
